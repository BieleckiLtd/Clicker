# Redesign handover — version 1.6.0

Original handover: 2026-10-10, Claude (Opus). Implementation and verification: 2026-10-10, Codex.

Latest deployment: 2026-10-15, Claude (Opus). Version 1.6.0 is a visual redesign: pure black, mostly monochrome, with YouTube, Netflix and Disney+ TV apps as the benchmarks. It is installed and running on `mytv` (10.0.0.125). All 9 channels, 22 playback positions and 22 History entries survived the update; 1.5.0 had 21 positions at its install. The TV reports Chromium 53 with a 1920×1080 window. `devicePixelRatio` reads 2, but the interface is drawn at 1920×1080: resolution media queries match 1, inspector captures are 1920×1080, and LG documents 1920×1080 graphics on UHD TVs, upscaled to the panel. Video uses a separate 4K plane. Evidence: `test-results/tv-state-after-1.6.0.json`, `test-results/tv-home-1.6.0.png` and `test-results/tv-motion-check-1.6.0.json`.

- **Performance on the TV.** The first 1.6.0 build was choppy on the C9: about 10 fps scrolling the grid, 17 fps opening the menu, 12–25 fps switching filters. Measured one effect at a time on the TV, the costs were: background artwork changing while moving (−15 fps or more), animated card zoom (21 fps with it, 44 without), card-by-card entrance (25 vs 43), the menu animating its width (36 vs 50), and a grid without its own GPU layer (37 vs 47). Fixes: the grid keeps `will-change: transform`; the rail opens instantly underneath the page, which slides aside to reveal it (only a transform animates); and the artwork updates only after focus rests for 650 ms. Browsers older than Chrome 80 (webOS 4.5 is 53) get `html.lite`: instant focus zoom, one fade for a new grid, and a half-resolution, pre-composited artwork layer without the slow zoom. After the fixes: 40–49 fps scrolling, 44 menu, 44 filters, with median frames at 16.7 ms. Measurements come from `requestAnimationFrame` timing over a remote inspector during scripted remote presses. Chrome 53 ignores `keyCode` in `new KeyboardEvent(...)`, so tests on the TV must dispatch a plain `Event` with `keyCode` set.
- **Focus ring and the final old-TV profile (same day).** Each card grid has one pre-drawn `.focus-ring` (its own layer) that glides to the focused thumbnail or channel card with a transform and zooms with it. The `.focused` class remains for state and tests. An intermediate build kept the TV smooth by removing the zoom, caption shift, chip widening and screen fades, but it lost the fluid feel, so they were restored on their own layers instead. On the TV, repainting is what drops frames, not moving pre-drawn layers. So `html.lite` (Chrome < 80) pre-composites what animates: card thumbnails and captions (`will-change: transform`; zoom ran at 32 fps without it, 50 with) and the channel chip row (widening at 48 → 51 fps). Small controls (chips, keyboard keys, player buttons) keep colour changes without zoom (44 vs 51 fps). A new grid fades in as one layer instead of card by card (25 vs 43 fps). Artwork is half-resolution, pre-composited and without the slow zoom. Screen fades cost nothing measurable and stay. Final TV figures with every animation on: grid moves 48 fps, channel chips 52, scrolling 49, menu 49, filters 50, Home ⇄ Search 53. Median frames are 16.7 ms, with at most two stalls per 3.6 s run. Key-to-frame is 8–24 ms, down from 25–45 ms.
- **Channel chips** centre their contents with flexbox. Text-baseline alignment had left the avatars slightly low; the TV now measures 0 px offset.
- **Video hidden behind the page (fixed).** webOS shows video on a hardware plane behind the page, through a hole the `<video>` cuts in the page. A fade-in on `#player` put the player into an offscreen layer, so the hole stopped at that layer and Home/Search showed over a playing video. The fade is gone. The browse screens are `visibility: hidden` during playback. A test now fails if anything around the video animates, fades, masks or is promoted to its own layer. Confirmed on the TV.
- **720p is not worth it.** `appinfo.json` can declare `"resolution": "1280x720"`. It is fixed per package and can't be switched at runtime. The app then scales itself to a 1280×720 window. A temporary 720p build measured on the TV with identical settings (artwork off): scrolling 48 vs 49 fps at 1080p, menu 53 vs 51, filters 51 vs 50. That is within noise, so drawing pixels is no longer the limit and 720p would only soften text. The shipped package stays at 1920×1080.

- **Look.** Black background, white focus and text, grey secondary text, and red (`#f4212e`) only for live indicators. Progress bars, the timeline and selected filters are white. Design tokens (colours, timing, easing) are CSS variables at the top of `app/style.css`.
- **Navigation rail.** A 112 px icon rail. When it has focus it opens to 300 px with labels underneath the page (the page has `z-index: 2` and a black background), and the page slides 188 px to the right and dims, revealing the labels. Only the page's transform and opacity animate, and the page is never covered. Settings is pinned at the bottom.
- **Home.** Same four-column grid with no hero. Cards are larger (about 400 px wide), and the focused card zooms 6% with a white ring. Behind the header, **background artwork** from the focused video fades in with a slow zoom, under dark gradients. Remote focus keeps the focused row at the top. The grid scrolls smoothly: `scrollTop` is set immediately and only the visual move is animated, so focus logic and tests read final values at once. Filter, History and Home changes play a row-by-row entrance. Background data updates wait for an entrance animation to finish instead of cutting it off.
- **Search.** Search icon, blinking caret, rounded keys that zoom on focus, the same chips and cards as Home, and channel cards with an action pill.
- **Player.** Rebuilt layout. A round Back button sits at the top left. At the bottom are the LIVE / audience pill, title, channel line, timeline (elapsed time left, length or LIVE right) and one control row: Play / Pause (round) on the left; Captions, Post & comments and Quality (showing the current resolution, e.g. "1080p") on the right. Quality and Captions menus open upward from the bottom right; the current option has a check mark. The remote-key hint lines are gone, including the one in the Post & comments panel and in Settings. The Stats for nerds button is gone; the panel remains on the Blue key for troubleshooting. Focus zones run top to bottom: Back → timeline (starts here; Left / Right seek, OK pauses) → control row. The controls fade and slide away when hidden. Panels slide or drop in. The title stops short of the Post & comments panel when it is open.
- **Captions** (new, `app/captions.js`). X lists subtitle tracks (often "en (auto-generated)") in the video's HLS master playlist even when the app plays the MP4. The catalog keeps that playlist as `captionSource`. The player reads the subtitle tracks, downloads the public WebVTT (one file for typical uploads, at most 40 segments), strips X's word-timing tags and draws the cues itself in an escaped overlay above the controls (lower when the controls are hidden). In a 2026-10-15 sample, 2 of 11 recent videos from the saved channels had captions. The chosen language, or Off, is stored as the `captions` setting and applied to the next video with that language. Requests are cancelled when the video changes or closes. The caption files return CORS headers for the browser origins tested; the TV file origin has not been tried yet.
- **Vertical videos** are named and capped by their short side, so a 1080×1920 upload is "1080p" and is now chosen. Before, it was labelled 1920p and skipped by the 1080p cap, so 480×852 played.
- **Settings** (rail, bottom). Two switches: **Animations** (transitions, focus zoom, smooth scrolling, entrances) and **Background artwork**. Both are on by default; Animations starts off when the system prefers reduced motion. Settings are stored in the existing DB8 record with a localStorage mirror, so they survive package updates. Up / Down chooses, OK switches, Back or Left closes. With Animations off, `html.motion` is removed and every transition, zoom and entrance is gone, except loading spinners. With artwork off, `html.artwork` is removed and no artwork is loaded.
- Styles that need motion or artwork are scoped under `html.motion` / `html.artwork`, so the base styles are already the simple version. Animations use only `transform` and `opacity`. The grid fades at its top and bottom edges using `-webkit-mask-image`, which applies only with Animations on.
- 76 checks, including the video-plane guard and the focus ring, plus new tests for Settings persistence (DB8), switching Settings by remote, row anchoring and the artwork backdrop, captions (track parsing, cue text, escaping, remembering the choice, videos without captions), the new player focus order, and portrait quality naming. Screenshots for review were taken in local Chromium at 1920×1080 with live public data.

## Product and source constraints

X TV browses public channels and plays their videos and broadcasts directly on the TV, without a PC content server or X login. Search matches channel names and handles, then aggregates recent media; it does not search every X post by keyword. Public-source availability can vary. Errors and partial results are displayed rather than reported as an empty successful search.

No account tokens, cookies, or credentialed X requests were added. The source investigation remains in [x-policy-check.md](x-policy-check.md).

## Browsing and search

- Home uses a four-column grid on the 1920×1080 canvas, with a fixed header and scrolling cards. The icon rail has its own space; when it opens it moves the page aside instead of covering it (1.6.0). The local update replaces For you with Hot, alongside Live, Videos, and saved channels. Hot includes live streams and the past 24 hours of saved-channel videos, ranked by views divided by `(ageHours + 2)^1.5`. Older videos/replays remain in Videos and channel filters.
- The app version is a subdued label at the bottom left of the sidebar. The redundant “Your channels” heading was removed. The Live filter has a red recording dot when a resolved live broadcast is present; the dot is muted when no live content is known. Live cards also have a recording bullet.
- More than four saved channels use compact avatar filters. Hovering or remote focus reveals the title. Labels remain available to accessibility tools, and a missing avatar falls back to the app icon. Four or fewer channels keep their titles visible.
- Search has a TV keyboard on the left and a three-column results grid on the right, with All / Live / Videos / Channels filters. Search progress is visible, matching channels appear before slow timelines complete, and channels without media can still be saved.
- Up to eight channels are matched; recent media from the first six are aggregated, deduplicated, and sorted live first, then newest. The first six pending broadcasts are resolved with two concurrent requests. Unknown or failed broadcasts are not incorrectly labelled live/replay.
- Query changes immediately invalidate old callbacks. Debouncing is 700 ms. Empty and short queries show guidance; partial and total request failures have distinct messages.
- Remote navigation moves between sidebar, keyboard, filters, and cards. The delete key uses an SVG icon. Back closes panels/player/Search in order and restores the previously selected card/filter. Pending opens and recovery requests are cancelled when leaving.

## Favourites and resume

TV favourites and playback positions use an app-owned webOS DB8 kind, with a localStorage fallback. Existing localStorage favourites migrate into DB8. Serialized writes and startup merge guards prevent rapid edits or late database reads from losing the latest state. The desktop preview has its own independent localStorage state.

Videos and broadcast replays resume from their saved position. Progress is saved every five seconds, on player exit, and on app hide/page exit. Completion means the final three seconds, or final 5% for short clips. Completed videos restart from zero while retaining their Watched marker. Live streams open live. Cursor clicks on the timeline seek to that point and continue playback.

Local update: thumbnails show progress bars and percentage/Watched labels in Home, Search, and History. The sidebar History menu lists all retained played videos and broadcasts, with All / In progress / Watched filters and the latest playback first. Playback must actually start before a new entry is recorded. The existing app-owned DB8 record now contains history metadata and completion flags, with a localStorage mirror and timestamp merges for startup races. The previous 80-position cap is removed. Old saved positions become history entries; available catalogue metadata enriches them, otherwise a generic Video/Broadcast title is used. Already-pruned positions and previously unrecorded live sessions cannot be reconstructed.

History stores stable post/broadcast IDs and display metadata, excluding playback URLs and qualities. Opening a history clip fetches `/2/status/{id}` for fresh formats; broadcasts go through the existing resolver. Unavailable entries remain visible with an error. Leaving History for Search or pressing Back cancels a pending open. Closing playback updates thumbnail progress and preserves the selected filter/card.

## Player, quality, and recovery

- Bundled hls.js uses Media Source Extensions on the older TV, with native HLS as a fallback. This addresses broadcasts that silently fail in the TV's native HLS player. HLS master playlists and all source-provided quality options are retained, including 2160p / 4K where supplied. The default is the highest available resolution up to 1080p.
- During a playing HLS stream, a quality change uses hls.js's conservative loading switch. The existing stream and buffer continue playing while the requested quality loads; the quality label shows the pending choice until its fragment is playing. This uses one video decoder. MP4 changes and paused HLS changes reload while preserving position and pause state.
- Failed/stalled playback automatically tries recovery, with a maximum of three app attempts: refresh a broadcast link once and try available lower qualities. Position and pause intent are preserved. Back and manual quality selection cancel pending recovery. If a requested HLS quality fails while the current buffer remains usable, playback keeps the existing quality.
- Loading and final failure messages are mutually exclusive. A late waiting event cannot restore Loading over the final error. Buffered paused playback no longer leaves a stale Loading label.
- Stats for nerds (Blue key only since 1.6.0) shows decoded resolution, source bitrate/codec, buffer ahead, dropped frames, and player state. Source bitrate is not an internet speed measurement.
- Post & comments displays full post text and paginated public replies while playback continues. Errors have Retry; additional pages have Load more. Text is escaped before emoji rendering.
- Player toolbar buttons use SVG icons, rounded dark backgrounds, and a clear remote focus state. Scrollbars use the dark theme. Bundled static Noto fonts and local Twemoji SVGs cover symbols, accented text, and emoji such as 🪩 without relying on missing TV glyphs.

## Remote controls

| Control | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Up / Down | Move between the Back button, timeline and control row; move through an open Quality or Captions menu; scroll the open post panel |
| Left / Right | Seek ten seconds on the timeline, choose a button in the control row, select Post / Comments / Chat tabs, or (Left) close an open menu |
| OK | Pause / play on the timeline; press the selected button or menu option; load more/retry comments |
| Red | Quality |
| Green | Play / pause |
| Yellow | Post & comments |
| Blue | Stats for nerds (no on-screen button) |
| Back | Hide the entire visible overlay, including active panels/menus; the next press closes playback. Holding the key cannot perform both steps. Back from History or Search returns to Home. |
| On-screen Back | Close playback immediately, save progress, and restore the selected card; pointer click and remote OK behave the same. |
